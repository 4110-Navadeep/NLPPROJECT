NLP Methodology & Machine Learning Approach

1. Text Preprocessing & Negation Preservation: Maintenance text is normalized through contraction expansion (e.g. "wont" → "will not") and domain abbreviation normalization (e.g. "corr" → "corrosion", "temp" → "temperature"). Critical negation terms (no, not, never, unable, failed) are explicitly preserved because their absence fundamentally alters failure semantics (e.g. "will not start" vs "start").

2. TF-IDF Feature Extraction: Observations are vectorized using unigrams and bigrams with sublinear term-frequency scaling. This captures both standalone diagnostic keywords (e.g. "vibration", "leak") and crucial phrasal combinations (e.g. "no power", "over heating", "high resistance").

3. Class Imbalance Mitigation: Across the 22 categories, frequency ranges from over 100 samples (Minor in-service problems) down to single-instance classes. Inverse-frequency class weighting (class_weight='balanced') ensures the optimization algorithm penalizes misclassifications on rare failure categories.

4. Model Explainability & Limitations: Linear model weights enable transparent term contribution decomposition ($w_{k,j} \cdot x_j$). The system includes low-confidence safeguards when probabilities fall below 30% or when alternative categories exhibit close margins. Statistical predictions support technician decision-making and do not replace physical verification.

Project Overview

In contemporary manufacturing, unscheduled machinery breakdowns cause severe economic losses, hazardous factory environments, and substantial material and energetic waste. While modern facilities collect rich unstructured maintenance logs, technicians frequently record symptoms as brief written observations. This application demonstrates an explainable, NLP-driven machine learning system that ingests technician reports, predicts equipment failure categories, explains word-level contributions, and generates formal downloadable PDF reports.

Unlike black-box neural networks, this architecture utilizes calibrated Logistic Regression coefficients and TF-IDF term weights to provide full mathematical explainability, ensuring engineers understand exactly which symptoms and phrases influenced the diagnostic outcome before initiating physical repair procedures.

Core Project Modules

Module 1

Maintenance Report Processing & NLP Features

Ingests natural English inspection observations. Expands contractions, normalizes technical abbreviations, strictly preserves negation tokens (no, not, never, unable, failed), and generates sublinear TF-IDF n-gram feature representations.

Module 2

Machine Learning Equipment Failure Prediction

Trains a Multinomial Logistic Regression classifier with balanced inverse-frequency class weights on the FMC-MWO2KG benchmark dataset, predicting 22 granular failure categories with probability scores and low-confidence warnings.

Module 3

Explainability, Guidance & PDF Reporting

Decomposes linear decision scores into individual word contributions, pairs predictions with targeted inspection checklists, and compiles formal downloadable engineering PDF reports via ReportLab.

Sustainable Maintenance Pillars
1. Reducing Unscheduled Downtime:

Early triage of reported symptoms avoids catastrophic emergency shutdowns and minimizes production line halts.

2. Extending Machinery Lifespan:

Targeted inspection of identified subsystems (seals, bearings, cooling) prevents progressive degradation of heavy mechanical capital.

3. Minimizing Resource Waste:

Rapid identification of leakage, overheating, and electrical tripping limits fluid spillage and wasted electrical drive energy.

4. Explainable Decision Support:

Feature contribution transparency empowers human technicians to verify diagnostic reasoning before undertaking invasive interventions.
